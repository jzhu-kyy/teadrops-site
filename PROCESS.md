# Process notes

## Design intent

The homepage begins with the people and landscape behind the product rather than with packaging. A full-bleed view of the Thái Nguyên tea fields establishes origin and small-batch craft before introducing Peach Black Tea. Dark forest tones, warm peach accents, rounded controls, canvas texture, and restrained editorial typography connect the agricultural story to Teadrops' grounded visual identity.

The hero uses slow camera movement and isolated micro-movements around the three harvesters to suggest the act of picking tea without turning the scene into an attention-heavy animation. Motion is removed when a visitor enables reduced-motion preferences.

## User flow

1. Discover the new Peach Black Tea and its origin.
2. Enter the shop through a short editorial film that moves from handpicking to blending tea leaf with peach, then hands off directly to the collection.
3. Browse the wider tea collection and filter by tea type.
4. Open a product-specific detail page with its own imagery, tasting notes, brewing guidance, and provenance.
5. Select a bundle or add-on.
6. Review quantities and totals in the cart.

The shop film was rebuilt as a lightweight HTML/CSS sequence rather than an embedded video. It borrows the reference recording's editorial grid, full-frame imagery, paced statements, and final product reveal while using Teadrops' own tea-garden and Peach Black Tea assets. A visible skip control and a shortened reduced-motion version keep the sequence optional and accessible.

## Generative AI disclosure

Generative AI was used to transform a user-provided Vietnamese tea-garden reference image into the oil-painted homepage hero asset, to create and refine the matching illustrated tea-house interior and its animated teapot assets, to produce the six ceramic-jar product illustrations used across the shop, and to assist with front-end implementation and iteration. The information hierarchy, interaction flow, visual selection, copy editing, and final design decisions were reviewed and directed by the designer.

The original reference image is retained locally for process documentation but excluded from the public repository. Before production use, all photographic source material should be replaced with a fully licensed or client-owned asset.
